# Terrain flow simulation: where the water goes, how fast, and whether 12 hours is possible

A 3D terrain page (`index.html`) that routes snowmelt and rain across Denver
Water's South Platte collection system on real terrain, shows how the single
NOAA rain gauge records, applies the physics that actually applies (Manning,
Bernoulli, degree-day melt), tests El Niño and warming against the long
records, and assesses whether a 12-hour TOC/alkalinity warning at Foothills is
feasible. The written assessment is **`FEASIBILITY.md`**.

Every number in the page carries a badge: **COMPUTED** from the files here,
**ASSUMED** (a literature-range constant, listed in `build_channels.py`
`ASSUMED`), or **GENERAL** knowledge not derived from these materials.

## Viewing

```
python3 serve.py            # from the repository root
open http://localhost:8765/teams/terrain-flow-sim/
```

Serve it rather than opening the file: it fetches JSON. Deep links:
`#flow`, `#gauge`, `#physics`, `#climate`, `#feasibility`. The main 3D map has
a "Terrain flow simulation" button that lands here.

Needs a network connection for satellite imagery (Esri), terrain tiles
(Mapzen/AWS) and the MapLibre library. Everything else is committed. The page
degrades gracefully: any missing bundle shows a note naming the script that
regenerates it, and the other tabs keep working. Missing `sim-data/d8-grid.json`
disables the particle simulation only.

## What is real and what is a cartoon

- **Real terrain, real routing.** Particles follow D8 steepest descent on the
  DEM and join the NHDPlus mainstem; river speed is Manning velocity for that
  day's flow tier; tunnel and conduit times are Bernoulli/Darcy lower bounds.
- **Real forcings.** Seeding is proportional to each basin's daily melt (SWE
  lost at its pillows) plus the NOAA gauge's rain.
- **Cartoon.** Overland speed is illustrative; the reservoir dwell is drawn as
  one day (nominal residence is 5–24 days, real short-circuiting ~4 h); particle
  counts are a picture of routing, not a volume; Blue River water beyond Roberts
  Tunnel follows a straight line (no North Fork flowline is committed); Fraser
  and Williams Fork water is counted at its tunnel and dropped (it serves the
  Moffat system, not Foothills).

## Hygiene

- Denver Water's originals in `data/`, `scripts/`, `figures/`, `reference/`
  are untouched. Their terms are copied here as `TERMS.md`, `sim-data/TERMS.md`
  and `results/TERMS.md` and travel with any derived file.
- All readings are provisional; the page and memo say so.
- Known artefact handled: Michigan Creek SWE 9.0 on 12–15 May 2026 is masked
  via the explainable-viz masks; the models use Hoosier Pass.
